# Visual devices

Devices that well-made animated explainers use, in our own words, as a menu to choose from. Never copy the scenes, characters or layouts of another film.

## Devices

| # | Device | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Persistent instrument**: a readout of the key quantity stays in a fixed corner while everything else changes. Examples: a depth badge counting from 257 m to 4005 m, a pressure gauge, a depth pin moving along a route map. | One quantity rises or falls through a stretch of the film. |
| 2 | **Scale through a familiar inset**: a round inset puts a known object next to the unknown one, e.g. a stack of cars for the pressure on a diver. | A size, weight or amount is hard to imagine. |
| 3 | **Reveal with light**: a searchlight cone uncovers the subject. Only what is lit exists, and labels appear inside the cone. | The main subject, or a hidden part of it, first appears; exploration. |
| 4 | **Before and after in one layout**: the same leaderboard re-sorts and its numbers count up; only the change moves. | A change or a ranking is the point. |
| 5 | **Counting made physical**: one icon stands for a stated unit (a legend says "= 100"). Icons pile into rows while a counter runs, then become columns with flags beside a map, one country at a time. | A number is the statement. |
| 6 | **Scale with reference marks**: a vertical km axis with known heights (cruising altitude, tropopause) that the subject grows past; a dimension line across a crater. | A value needs a yardstick. |
| 7 | **The model builds up and returns**: a grid plane gains a time axis and becomes a cube, one step per statement. Later the same cube returns with changed conditions (tilted "now" slices). A flat maze returns as a 3D maze cube. | Cause and effect, a "what if", or a concept built from simpler parts. |
| 8 | **Split panels**: two or three cases side by side. Variants: comic cut-outs with wobbly bright borders and date pills, or a horizontal split for a race. | Equal cases are compared, or two places are shown at the same moment. |
| 9 | **Historical anchor**: a small dated caption (1997, Kasparov vs. Deep Blue) grounds a development in an event the viewer knows; a year pill dates a scene. | A trend needs a starting point the viewer already knows. |
| 10 | **Same staging for every candidate**: each subject stands on the same lit pedestal, so only what differs draws the eye (the brains of different animals, then of early humans). | Several items are compared on one property. |
| 11 | **Scale by multiplication**: one item becomes rows that fill the frame. | "Many", "millions" or repetition is the point. |
| 12 | **A face for the system**: an abstract system becomes a simple character (a scoring bowl with a mouth, a screen with a face) so the viewer can follow what it does. | A process inside a machine needs an actor. Keep it a tool: for AI, imply no feelings or consciousness. |
| 13 | **Home base**: a detailed hub (a treehouse, a workshop full of formulas, books and gadgets) that the film returns to between chapters. | A film has several chapters. The detail rewards rewatching while the foreground stays clear. |
| 14 | **In-world screen**: a device inside the scene (a tablet on a robot arm, a terminal) shows the close-up or the data. | A detail or a number needs a close-up without leaving the world. |
| 15 | **Caveat in the picture**: a short note admits a simplification ("only true as a thought experiment") or points to the sources. | The picture simplifies, or a claim needs a source. |

## Places and colour across a film

These films get most of their compositions by camera travel and action inside a few connected worlds, with crafted transitions between chapters, not by hard cuts to new designs. Count compositions, not places: a 40 s stretch can hold ten compositions in three or four places. Each place has its own dominant hue, and most are bright. A place holds longer only while something travels through it and the view keeps changing, such as a submarine crossing a map while its depth labels change.

## Labels and type

- Place and zone names sit in saturated pills (magenta, violet) at the top centre.
- Values stick to their object: a pill on the route pin, a speed under a moving ship, a depth readout in a fixed corner.
- Group names sit in a small dark pill under the subject.
- Labels are mostly in sentence case. Capitals appear in game interfaces and title banners.
- Most labels stay within four words; distances and caveats run longer.

## Transitions and camera

- Circle wipe into the next scene.
- Whip into a character's eye.
- A hand rises into an empty sky, then the camera finds its owner.
- Pull back from the event to the whole planet with a marker.
- Dive into a circuit board to go inside the machine.

## Colour and mood

- One dominant hue per scene, shifted for drama: a green idyll turns orange and red as a fireball grows.
- Dark scenes (space, deep sea, machine interiors) carry saturated accents and glowing light sources.
- A style switch marks "inside the program": pixel interface, game windows, error panels.

These devices sit on top of the look in the preset's `STYLE.md` and the grammar in [composition.md](composition.md). Each concept names the devices it uses and why. Use what the beats need; no single device carries the whole film.
