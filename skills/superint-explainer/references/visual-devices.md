# Visual devices from reference contact sheets

Derived on 2026-10-31 from ten contact sheets of Kurzgesagt films on other topics (one frame every 2 s; the sheet numbers below refer to them; local copies are references of the `flat-explainer` preset, added with `superint-video preset reference`). The sheets are copyrighted. Keep them out of version control, and never copy their scenes, characters or layouts. This file keeps the principles, in our own words, as devices to choose from.

## Devices

| # | Device | Seen in (sheet, time) | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Persistent instrument**: a readout of the key quantity stays in a fixed corner while everything else changes. Examples: a depth badge counting from 257 m to 4005 m, a pressure gauge, a depth pin moving along a route map. | 09 (01:40–02:18), 10 (06:44–06:50) | One quantity rises or falls through a stretch of the film. |
| 2 | **Scale through a familiar inset**: a round inset puts a known object next to the unknown one, e.g. a stack of cars for the pressure on a diver. | 09 (02:26–02:28) | A size, weight or amount is hard to imagine. |
| 3 | **Reveal with light**: a searchlight cone uncovers the subject. Only what is lit exists, and labels appear inside the cone. | 09 (01:52–02:02), 10 (07:00–07:28); as a reward beam in 08 (04:26–04:34) | The main subject, or a hidden part of it, first appears; exploration. |
| 4 | **Before and after in one layout**: the same leaderboard re-sorts and its numbers count up; only the change moves. | 07 (03:32, 03:46–03:50) | A change or a ranking is the point. |
| 5 | **Counting made physical**: one icon stands for a stated unit (a legend says "= 100"). Icons pile into rows while a counter runs, then become columns with flags beside a map, one country at a time. | 05 (00:22–00:36) | A number is the statement. |
| 6 | **Scale with reference marks**: a vertical km axis with known heights (cruising altitude, tropopause) that the subject grows past; a dimension line across a crater. | 06 (03:02–03:18) | A value needs a yardstick. |
| 7 | **The model builds up and returns**: a grid plane gains a time axis and becomes a cube, one step per statement. Later the same cube returns with changed conditions (tilted "now" slices). A flat maze returns as a 3D maze cube. | 03 (01:50–02:28), 04 (02:44–03:12); mazes 07 (04:04–04:08), 08 (04:54–04:58) | Cause and effect, a "what if", or a concept built from simpler parts. |
| 8 | **Split panels**: two or three cases side by side. Variants: comic cut-outs with wobbly bright borders and date pills, or a horizontal split for a race. | 01 (01:22–01:30), 03 (01:42–01:44), 04 (02:52–03:18) | Equal cases are compared, or two places are shown at the same moment. |
| 9 | **Historical anchor**: a small dated caption (1997, Kasparov vs. Deep Blue) grounds a development in an event the viewer knows; a year pill dates a scene. | 02 (04:52–04:54), 07 (03:54–03:56) | A trend needs a starting point the viewer already knows. |
| 10 | **Same staging for every candidate**: each subject stands on the same lit pedestal, so only what differs draws the eye (the brains of different animals, then of early humans). | 01 (01:20, 01:46–01:48) | Several items are compared on one property. |
| 11 | **Scale by multiplication**: one item becomes rows that fill the frame. | 08 (04:12–04:18) | "Many", "millions" or repetition is the point. |
| 12 | **A face for the system**: an abstract system becomes a simple character (a scoring bowl with a mouth, a screen with a face) so the viewer can follow what it does. | 02 (04:52–05:04), 08 (04:20–04:42) | A process inside a machine needs an actor. Keep it a tool: for AI, imply no feelings or consciousness. |
| 13 | **Home base**: a detailed hub (a treehouse, a workshop full of formulas, books and gadgets) that the film returns to between chapters. | 05 (00:00–00:20, 00:38–00:42) | A film has several chapters. The detail rewards rewatching while the foreground stays clear. |
| 14 | **In-world screen**: a device inside the scene (a tablet on a robot arm, a terminal) shows the close-up or the data. | 10 (06:52–06:58), 02 (04:44–04:50) | A detail or a number needs a close-up without leaving the world. |
| 15 | **Caveat in the picture**: a short note admits a simplification ("only true as a thought experiment") or points to the sources. | 04 (02:54), 07 (04:02) | The picture simplifies, or a claim needs a source. |

## Places and colour across a film

The sheets show many compositions, but the films get most of them by camera travel and action inside a few connected worlds, with crafted transitions between chapters, not by hard cuts to new designs. Count compositions, not places. Sheet 01 runs through about ten compositions in 40 s: animal heads on lit pedestals, a raven in a jungle, an octopus on a reef, a wolf on a snowy ridge, birds against a yellow sky, a hand with a bone, an ape in a desert, early humans on pedestals, hands knapping a stone. Sheet 05 goes from a treehouse to a lab, a yellow counting ground, a world map, the lab again and space in 48 s. Each place has its own dominant hue, and most are bright. A place holds longer only while something travels through it and the view keeps changing: in 09 the submarine crosses the shelf map for 22 s while depth labels change.

## Labels and type

- Place and zone names sit in saturated pills (magenta, violet) at the top centre.
- Values stick to their object: a pill on the route pin, a speed under a moving ship, a depth readout in a fixed corner.
- Group names sit in a small dark pill under the subject.
- Labels are mostly in sentence case. Capitals appear in game interfaces and title banners.
- Most labels stay within four words; distances and caveats run longer.

## Transitions and camera

- Circle wipe into the next scene (06, 02:34).
- Whip into a character's eye (04, 02:32–02:34).
- A hand rises into an empty sky, then the camera finds its owner (01, 01:34–01:40).
- Pull back from the event to the whole planet with a marker (06, 02:56–03:00).
- Dive into a circuit board to go inside the machine (02, 05:18).

## Colour and mood

- One dominant hue per scene, shifted for drama: a green idyll turns orange and red as a fireball grows (06, 02:36–02:54).
- Dark scenes (space, deep sea, machine interiors) carry saturated accents and glowing light sources.
- A style switch marks "inside the program": pixel interface, game windows, error panels (02, 05:10–05:16; 08, 04:44–04:46).

These devices sit on top of the look in the preset's `STYLE.md` and the grammar in [composition.md](composition.md). Each concept names the devices it uses and why. Use what the beats need; no single device carries the whole film.
