# The standard for product films

Adapted from cinetic (Leonxlnx, MIT): its SKILL.md standard, hard bans and `references/copy-and-type.md`, rewritten for Superint Video. Numbers are at 1080p unless they say otherwise. Where the brand or the preset's `STYLE.md` sets a look rule (type face, colours), it wins over the house defaults marked *default* here.

## Copy

**The launch formula.**

1. **What it is**, as line 1 (or line 2 after one short hook), on screen by 3 s: "[Name] is a [plain category] for [who]." or "[Name] turns [input] into [output]."
2. **What it does**, 3–5 lines, one concrete thing each, said with a verb, while the UI shows exactly that thing.
3. **The close:** the name and what to do ("Tessel is out now." with `tessel.app` under the name).

**The update formula.** "[Name] [version] is out." first, one line per change as a result the user sees ("Approvals take one tap."), then the name and version on the end card.

| Format | Lines | Words a line | In all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Launch film | 5–8 | 2–7 (sweet spot 4–6) | about 20–45 |
| Update film | 4–6 | 2–6 | about 12–30 |
| Product or feature video | 3–6 | 2–7 | up to 35 |
| Feature loop | 1–2 | 2–6 | up to 12 |
| Logo sting | the name + at most 1 | 2–5 | the name + 5 |

**The line test.** Every line passes all six:

1. A stranger understands it with the sound off, without knowing the product.
2. It names a concrete thing the product does (or what it is), with a verb.
3. One sentence of 2–7 words with one claim: never a keyword, a label or a list.
4. No metaphor, slogan shape, praise adjective or fancy word.
5. The product's UI shows exactly that thing while the line is on screen.
6. It is still true in a year (no "new", "introducing"); an update film's version line is the one exception.

**Voice.** Plain words a customer would say out loud. Nouns, verbs, numbers; a number beats an adjective ("Builds finish in 40 seconds."). Present tense, active, one verb doing the work. The name in the what-it-is line and on the end card; "It" in between. Sentence case; no exclamation marks, questions, emoji or suspense ellipses.

**Budget.** At most about 2.4 words a second in any 5 s, about 1.6 over the body. No text in at least 15 % of the frames: the product needs room to act.

**Strings the product shows** (a typed command, a URL, an app label, code) are the product talking, not lines. They follow the same truth rule: real, readable, held long enough when the story needs them read.

### Cut on sight

These are landing-page grammar and the fastest tells of generated work. A word the brand's own voice owns may stay, recorded in `brief.md`.

- **Labels:** eyebrow or kicker labels above a headline ("NEW FEATURE", "01 / SPEED"), stacked taglines, "Introducing…".
- **Keyword piles:** one- or two-word lines that claim nothing ("Speed." "Total control."), three-item lists ("Plan. Track. Win.").
- **Text walls:** two lines readable at once (a planned swap excepted), paragraphs, bullets, a line over 7 words.
- **Filler:** decorative mono captions, fake metrics, version strings, coordinates and timestamps nobody reads, lorem ipsum.
- **Openers:** Meet, Say hello to, Ready to…?, What if…?, Imagine…, One more thing.
- **Claims:** the future of, reimagined, redefined, next-gen, revolutionary, game-changing, like never before.
- **Verbs:** unleash, unlock, supercharge, streamline, elevate, empower, revolutionize, level up, harness.
- **Metaphors:** magic, superpower, second brain, rocket fuel, on autopilot, at your fingertips, copilot.
- **Adjectives:** seamless, effortless, powerful, smart, intelligent, AI-powered, all-in-one.

## Picture

1. **Words in the middle.** A statement is centred and sits in the middle band of the frame (its centre at 35–65 % of the height) for most of its time. Never flush left at the top or bottom. "Text on top, big picture below" at most once a film.
2. **One element carried.** Every scene change hands one element on: it grows, swells, flies, converges or becomes the next form. Never a cut from a line to an unrelated card, never the line-card-line-card loop, never an empty frame between scenes. See [structure.md](structure.md).
3. **The picture tells what the product does.** Each picture proves its line without repeating its words: input becoming output in steps, a count rising, "every" drawn as many at once. With the words covered, every picture beat still reads. Data agrees when paused: counts and times that fit together, a face with its own name.
4. **Text with care.** Lines arrive in the film's one face, the way each line enters changing line by line (build, blur-in, mask, slide), never two lines in a row alike. Big: a line alone at about 9 % of the frame height cap, never under 6.5 %; the claim bigger. Whole or in 2–3 chunks, never word by word. One type event per film (a giant word with the product rising behind it, a line an object pushes apart). Emphasis is quiet: the key word in the accent on at most two lines. Every line reads at 3:1 contrast against what stands behind it.
5. **Seams that flow.** Every hard cut hides itself: inside a blur that makes both sides unreadable, on an anchor held within 10 px, or in a whip that carries the picture. Never a cross-fade of two scenes. Two or three seam kinds a film, none on more than 40 % of the changes.
6. **Clean UI.** The product's real screens built in code at film size, every string its own. Left edges of stacked rows within 4 px, even inner padding, no text touching an edge, no accent bar down a card's side, widths measured, never guessed.
7. **Marks done right.** Other products' marks current and official, in one presentation a film (a chip in the line, pills around a centred line, or a wall). The end card grows from the film's last element, lands centred, keeps moving gently about its centre while the music rings out: the name once and one fact under it. The logo is the brand's own, never a sparkle or a generic glyph.
8. **Its own film.** One world for the film, one face, two or three moves, two or three seam kinds, one signature, one end-card design, declared in `direction.md`. A second film for the same product changes the world, the signature and the end card.
9. **Premium habits in every shot.** One sharp plane with a graded blur behind it; one camera move with a job, front-loaded and landing (else still: no float, drift or bob); light from a source, sized to it; a hero object in a real material; the product's real content in quantity; one scale jump (macro to wide); motion smear only on fast moves.
10. **Something moves with purpose in every frame.** A hold is the landing tail of a move, never still for more than 0.8 s, the end card included.

## Bans in a look you invent

Without a brand kit or a preset rule that says otherwise, these stay out (house defaults from cinetic):

- **Type:** serif faces, italic or fake oblique. One clean sans, plus a mono for code.
- **Colour:** neon; a monochrome accent wash; glassmorphism as a UI style; more than one dominant accent.
- **Decoration:** emoji and stock icons; sparkles for "AI", confetti, lens flares, code rain; bounce on anything that is not landing; springy overshoot on copy.
- **Pictures:** grey cards standing in for pictures; stock gestures (handshakes, pointing at screens); low-resolution or mixed-grade pictures; a person drawn in a crude style where the look calls for a photo.
- **Sound:** a film with no music; music ducked under effects.
